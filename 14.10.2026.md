**### Correction 1:** The customer is not always redirected only after the money is deducted



After the customer clicks Pay, Stripe processes the payment. The customer may be redirected to the merchant's success page, but that redirect does not prove that payment has succeeded.



For some payment methods, processing may still be pending when checkout completes. Your backend should check the actual payment status.



**### Correction 2:** `checkout.session.completed` is an event, not a message sent directly to Kafka



The actual flow is:



1\. Stripe sends the `checkout.session.completed` event to your webhook endpoint.

2\. Your Payment Integration System receives and verifies the event.

3\. Your service checks the payment status and updates its payment record.

4\. Your service publishes a payment event to Kafka, if your architecture uses Kafka.

5\. The Merchant Backend consumes that event and updates the order status.



Stripe does not automatically send its webhook event to your Kafka topic. Your application must publish the event to Kafka itself.



**### Correction 3:** Kafka does not automatically store all payment information



Kafka stores the messages your application publishes to its topics. For example, your service might publish:



```

{

&#x20; "eventType": "PAYMENT\_SUCCEEDED",

&#x20; "orderId": "ORD101",

&#x20; "paymentId": "PAY101",

&#x20; "status": "SUCCESS"

}

```



This is an illustrative application event, not the actual Stripe webhook payload.



You generally should not publish sensitive card details to Kafka. Store the necessary payment and order records in your database, and publish only the data required by downstream services.



**### Correction 4:** Money does not necessarily move directly from the customer's bank to the merchant's bank



For a typical card payment, Stripe and the card/payment networks coordinate authorization and processing. The merchant's Stripe balance and subsequent bank payout are separate parts of the flow. Actual settlement timing depends on the payment method and account.



* Synchronous communication — REST API

&#x20;       Merchant Backend → Stripe Provider Service → Stripe API → response containing the Checkout Session URL.



* Asynchronous communication — Webhook + Kafka

&#x20;       Stripe → Webhook → Payment Integration System → Kafka → Merchant Backend consumer.



* REST creates the checkout session, 
* the webhook reports the payment event, and 
* Kafka can distribute your application's payment result asynchronously to other services.
